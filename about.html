About the project

Access should not determine who gets to learn, make, or publish.

Open Frontier builds useful browser software and interactive explanations, then lowers the friction to using, understanding, and improving them.

Mission

Democratize useful software and hard knowledge. A learner should be able to explore quantum circuits without a laboratory. A small business should be able to make a credible website without a large budget. A developer should be able to inspect the source of the tool they use.

The practical version of that mission is simple: open a page, interact with the subject or tool, and leave with something useful. Reduce accounts, installs, paywalls, prerequisites, and hidden data flows wherever the product allows it.

The tools should be accessible. The knowledge should be inspectable. What people create should remain theirs.

The thesis

People learn difficult systems more deeply when they can manipulate them. A simulation that exposes parameters, intermediate states, and failure modes can make an abstract idea concrete. The same principle applies to software: a focused tool that runs immediately can be more empowering than a broad suite surrounded by onboarding and lock-in.

Open Frontier therefore combines two related bodies of work:

  • Interactive knowledge: simulations for robotics, quantum computing, cryptography, computer architecture, space systems, and biology.
  • Practical capability: tools and platforms for design, code, data, writing, marketing, music, finance, apps, games, websites, and AI.

What Open Frontier is

It is an ecosystem rather than one monolithic application. Each domain has its own storefront and repository structure. Products share a public philosophy and selected design conventions, while remaining operationally independent enough to evolve at different speeds.

Free knowledge stores

Self-contained simulations and tools, generally requiring no account, with a low-friction GitHub contribution model.

Platform stores

Apps, games, websites, and AI products with SDKs, publishing flows, hosting, identity, compliance, and optional backend services.

Pro products

Server-dependent capabilities such as hosted AI, persistent databases, storage, scheduling, collaboration, and custom domains.

Public documentation

Architecture, product comparisons, maturity assessments, roadmap, capability notes, and per-store references.

What it is not

  • It is not one uniform product with identical maturity across every storefront.
  • It is not currently a mature community foundation or a revenue-generating marketplace.
  • It is not a promise that every browser tool is offline-only or makes no network request.
  • It is not a substitute for professional medical, legal, financial, safety, or engineering advice.
  • It is not finished. Several products are actively changing, and some Pro experiences remain previews.

Principles

Free means useful

The free experience should solve a real problem, not merely demonstrate a paid product.

Immediate value

Do not require an account where a tool can work safely and meaningfully without one.

Local where practical

Prefer browser-side processing and storage for static tools, while documenting required external services.

Open contribution

Use inspectable formats, explicit licenses, public issue tracking, and small contribution units.

Honest maturity

Production, beta, scaffolded, and planned are not interchangeable labels.

Independent products

Share proven patterns without forcing every store into one internal architecture.

Free and Pro

Free and Pro are not always a feature gate around the same application. In some product lines they form a progression; in others they are architecturally different products.

  • Free browser products emphasize immediate access, open source, local execution, and low operating cost.
  • Pro platform products are intended to charge for server value: persistent compute, storage, collaboration, integrations, automation, and operational support.
  • ProWebStore is an exception to the shared subscription concept because each managed site carries independent infrastructure and deployment costs.

The project is currently pre-revenue. Pricing and creator payout mechanics described in documentation are the intended model, not a claim of established revenue or a large active subscriber base.

Current state

As of June 9, 2026, the strongest platform foundations are FreeAppStore, ProAppStore, FreeWebStore, FreeGameStore, and ProWebStore. The six simulation stores are live with 166 simulations. Design and marketing tool stores are live. FreeAgentStore and ProAgentStore are early beta. ProGameStore is scaffold-stage. Pro design and marketing products have meaningful scaffolding but are not complete public services.

For a structured review of maturity, risks, economics, and roadmap, read the stakeholder brief. For implementation detail, use the full documentation.

How the ecosystem is organized

Names use short internal codes in repositories and documentation. F generally means Free, P means Pro, and the remaining letters identify the product or subject: apps, games, web, agents, design, marketing, robotics, quantum, crypto, chips, space, biology, and other domains.

A storefront is the public catalog. A platform contains shared infrastructure such as SDKs, CLIs, Workers, compliance, and publishing. An item repository contains one app, game, site, agent, tool, or simulation.

Lifecycle and stewardship

Products move through concept, scaffold, preview, beta, production, maintenance, and possible retirement. Promotion should require a working core workflow, documented data flow, risk-appropriate tests, an owner, and a deployment path. Retirement should preserve source where licensing allows, explain alternatives, and avoid silently discarding user data.

Stewardship is currently founder-led. The desired direction is bounded ownership: trusted maintainers responsible for specific stores or capabilities, with public decisions and documented operational procedures.

Who is behind it

Open Frontier is currently led and primarily built by Serge, with AI-assisted development and a repository structure designed for broader contribution. The community ambition is ahead of the current community size; attracting repeat external contributors is an explicit next-stage goal.

How to participate

You can use and test products, report defects, improve accessibility or documentation, add a focused tool or simulation, contribute platform code, review a technical domain, or help establish partnerships and distribution. The contribution guide explains where each type of work fits.
